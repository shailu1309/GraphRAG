# Network Escalations - Team Page

## Who we are

Network Escalations is the second-line team for faults that frontline agents can't resolve on a call or chat. Wei Lin leads the team. We are a small group of senior advisors with a technical background. Most of us came up through frontline broadband support, and a couple came from field work.

We don't fix the network ourselves. Our job is to make sure a fault gets to the right engineering group with enough detail that nobody has to ring the customer back to ask the same questions twice. We also own the customer side of the fault until it is closed, which means updates, callbacks and confirming the service is working.

Core hours are 08:00 to 20:00 Monday to Friday and 09:00 to 17:00 on Saturday. Outside those hours, urgent cases go through the out-of-hours duty manager.

## What we handle

The Network Escalations team manages the NETESC queue. Raise a case to NETESC when:

- A broadband line has failed the standard remote tests twice and the customer has completed the in-home checks.
- There is intermittent loss of sync or speed degradation that has lasted more than 72 hours.
- Several customers in the same postcode area report the same fault within 24 hours. Flag this as a possible area issue.
- A mobile customer has no signal or persistent call drops at a fixed location, and the coverage checker shows the area as covered.
- A previous engineer visit did not resolve the fault.

Do **not** send these to NETESC:

- Billing disputes, including credits for outage time. These go to the billing team once the fault is closed.
- Equipment returns or replacements with no underlying fault.
- New installations that are still within the provisioning window.

## How to escalate

Cases without the basics get sent back. Before you raise a case, make sure it includes:

1. Account number and the service affected.
2. A clear fault description in plain words: what happens, when it started, and how often.
3. Results of all remote tests, with timestamps.
4. Confirmation of which in-home checks the customer completed. For example: router restarted, different filter tried, tested at master socket.
5. Customer availability for callbacks and any access restrictions for an engineer visit.
6. Any vulnerability flags on the account.

Set the priority honestly. "High" is reserved for total loss of service, customers with a vulnerability flag, and business accounts with a service-level commitment. Everything else is "Standard". Inflating priority slows the queue for everyone.

Our targets are:

- **High:** we pick up the case within 2 working hours and make first customer contact the same day.
- **Standard:** we pick up the case within 1 working day.

## Team routines and contacts

- **Daily stand-up, 08:45:** we review overnight cases, open area issues and anything close to breaching target.
- **Weekly review, Thursdays 14:00:** we look at aged cases (over 10 working days) and repeat escalations. Frontline team leaders are welcome to dial in.
- **Monthly:** we share a summary of common bounce-back reasons with frontline supervisors so coaching can be targeted.

If you have a question about a case you've already raised, add a note to the case rather than messaging an individual. Notes are seen by whoever picks it up, and direct messages get lost across shifts. For process questions or feedback about this page, contact a team supervisor through the usual team channel.
