# Customer Operations Monthly Review - May 2026

## Summary

This review covers April performance and actions agreed at the May review meeting. April was steady overall, with one clear improvement and two recurring problems. Handle time on NETESC improved. Outdated knowledge-base content and Monday staffing gaps continued to cause avoidable work.

Attendees: operations managers, team supervisors, a workforce planning analyst, a quality lead, and representatives from finance and CX Platforms. Minutes were circulated the same day. Actions are tracked in the shared action log.

## Performance Highlights

**NETESC handle time**
Wei Lin reported that NETESC handle time fell by 12% in April. The group discussed likely contributors:

- Fewer repeat transfers into the queue after the March refresher sessions.
- Better use of call notes at handoff, which cut time spent re-verifying details.
- Lower volume of complex cases late in the month. This may not repeat.

The group agreed not to treat the 12% as a new baseline until May and June figures confirm the trend. A supervisor will compare case mix month on month so the improvement is not overstated.

**Other queues**
Most other queues stayed within normal tolerance on answer rate and abandonment. No queue breached its service threshold for more than two consecutive days.

## Recurring Issues

**Outdated knowledge-base articles**
Outdated knowledge-base articles were again a top cause of escalations in April. This is the same finding as previous months. The quality lead noted these patterns from sampled escalations:

- Agents following steps that no longer match current processes.
- Articles that conflict with each other on the same topic.
- Missing review dates, which make it hard for agents to judge whether content is current.

Comments in the meeting were frank. Owners have been flagged before, but fixes have been slow because content review has no protected time. The group agreed to try a narrower approach. Only the articles most often cited in escalations will be reviewed, rather than attempting a full audit.

**Monday-morning understaffing**
Monday-morning understaffing continued in April, and Telvio took the overflow. Overflow kept wait times from spiking, but it adds cost and limits our visibility of case quality. Workforce planning will:

- Review Monday absence and late-start patterns over the last quarter.
- Test moving a small number of shifts earlier on Mondays.
- Report the overflow volume handled by Telvio each week in May.

## Commercial and Contracts

Finance raised concerns about variable charges in April. Maya Okafor asked CX Platforms to review every vendor overage clause. The review should identify:

- What triggers each overage.
- How each overage is calculated and invoiced.
- Any notice periods or caps we are not using.

CX Platforms confirmed receipt of the request and will report findings at a future review. No change to current arrangements is planned until that review is complete.
