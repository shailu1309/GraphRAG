# Workforce Management Q2 Plan

**From:** Sofia Marchetti, Workforce Management
**To:** Contact centre leadership group
**Status:** Draft for review at the next monthly operations meeting

## Purpose and scope

This memo sets out what Workforce Management will focus on in Q2. Sofia Marchetti manages the team. Most of the quarter goes on forecast accuracy, schedule adherence, and how shrinkage is planned. The memo covers scheduling for frontline agents only. Back-office and training staff rosters are not included and will be handled separately with the relevant managers.

Workforce Management schedules agents with Pulse. This plan does not propose changing the tool. It proposes changing how we use the forecasts Pulse produces, and how we fix them when we know they are wrong.

## Q1 review: what went wrong

Sofia Marchetti says Pulse forecasts underestimate Monday-morning demand. Supervisors reported the same thing all through Q1. Week after week, Monday queues built up before mid-morning while the schedule showed we had enough cover. Other days were mostly within tolerance.

The Monday shortfall had knock-on effects:

- Wait times rose in the first few hours of the week, and abandonment rose with them.
- Supervisors pulled agents off coaching and off-phone work to cover the queues, so those sessions slipped.
- Overtime was offered at short notice, often on Monday morning itself, and take-up was poor.
- Tuesday schedules were then built from a Monday that looked better than it was, because demand had been partly suppressed.

We have not yet pinned down the cause. Possible causes include a backlog of weekend contacts, payment and billing timing, and how the forecast weights recent weeks. Finding out which one it is forms part of this plan.

## Q2 priorities

**1. Monday forecast adjustment.** Until the root cause is understood, analysts will apply a manual uplift to Monday morning intervals.

- The uplift starts from the average Q1 variance between forecast and actual, and is reviewed every week.
- Each adjustment is recorded with a short reason so we can see whether it helps.

**2. Variance analysis.** An analyst will compare forecast against actual by interval for every Monday in Q1 and Q2. The output is a short findings note, due before the end of the quarter, saying whether we need a lasting change to forecast settings.

**3. Shift pattern trial.** We will trial moving some start times earlier on Mondays. This will be offered to volunteers first and agreed with team leaders, to avoid changing contracted hours without consultation.

**4. Shrinkage planning.** Coaching, one-to-ones and team meetings move away from Monday mornings by default. Supervisors keep the option to book them there if forecast cover allows.

**5. Intraday communication.** On Mondays, a member of the team will post interval updates to supervisors every hour until midday. Each update states the expected shortfall or surplus.

## Measures, governance and actions

We will judge progress on:

- Monday morning forecast accuracy against interval actuals
- Schedule adherence by team
- Short-notice overtime hours requested on Mondays
- Coaching sessions delivered against sessions planned

The team will review these every week in its own stand-up and report them monthly to leadership. If Monday accuracy has not clearly improved by mid-quarter, we will revisit the uplift method.
