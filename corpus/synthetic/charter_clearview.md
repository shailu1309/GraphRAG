# Project Charter: Clearview

## Purpose and Objective

Project Clearview exists because a large share of inbound contact is generated by the customer bill itself. Agents regularly report calls where the customer is not disputing a charge, only trying to work out what it is: pro-rated amounts after a plan change, one-off fees, add-ons billed in arrears, and credits that appear on a different line from the charge they offset.

The objective of Project Clearview is to redesign customer bills so that 30% fewer customers call about charges they do not understand.

The baseline will be taken from the call reason codes agents select at wrap-up, using the "charge not understood" category and its sub-codes. The baseline covers the three full billing cycles before any redesigned bill is issued. We will measure the reduction over the three full billing cycles after the redesigned bill reaches all customers. We will not count calls about genuine billing errors toward the target. Those will be tracked separately so the redesign is not credited or blamed for them.

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Layout, wording and ordering of the monthly bill for broadband and mobile customers, in both the PDF and online versions.
- Plain-language descriptions for each charge type, including pro-rata, one-off and usage charges.
- A short "what changed since last month" summary at the top of the bill.
- Updated agent guidance and knowledge articles that match the new bill layout.
- A controlled pilot with a sample of customers before full rollout.

**Out of scope:**
- Changes to pricing, tariffs or the charges themselves.
- Changes to billing cycles or payment methods.
- Collections letters and final-notice correspondence.
- Rebuilding the statement data model. The project works with the data as it exists today.

## Ownership, Dependencies and Governance

Daniel Reyes owns Project Clearview and is accountable for scope decisions, the pilot go/no-go and final sign-off of the bill design.

Project Clearview depends on the Relay CRM for statement data. Every line on the redesigned bill must be traceable to a field available from the Relay CRM. If a proposed description needs data the Relay CRM does not currently provide, the item is logged as a dependency risk and goes to the project owner. It is not worked around with manual data.

Supporting input is expected from:
- the billing operations team, on charge logic and edge cases
- a team leader from the contact centre floor, on real customer questions
- the legal and compliance team, on mandatory bill content
- the quality team, on reason-code accuracy

**Cadence:** a fortnightly working session, and a monthly status note to contact centre management covering progress, risks and baseline figures.

## Milestones, Risks and Open Actions

**Milestones (indicative):**
1. Baseline call-reason data agreed and signed off.
2. Charge-description library drafted and reviewed.
3. Draft bill design tested with a small group of agents and customers.
4. Pilot issued to a sample of customers for two billing cycles.
5. Go/no-go decision and full rollout.
6. Post-rollout measurement against the 30% target.

**Key risks:**
- Inconsistent wrap-up coding makes the baseline unreliable. *Mitigation:* a refresher for agents and a quality-team spot check before the baseline is locked.
- Statement data gaps limit what the bill can explain. *Mitigation:* review data fields early, and log any gap as a dependency.
- Agents are unfamiliar with the new layout at launch. *Mitigation:* publish knowledge articles before the pilot bills are sent.
