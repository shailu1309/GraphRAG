# Quality Assurance Review - Q1 2026

**Prepared by:** Quality Assurance
**Review owner:** Tom Becker, Quality Assurance lead
**Period covered:** 1 January – 31 March 2026
**Distribution:** Contact centre management, team supervisors, training team

## Summary

This review covers call and chat quality across the contact centre for Q1 2026. Overall evaluation scores held steady against Q4 2025. Two areas stood out. Escalation handling improved after targeted training for Tier 1 agents. Repeat contacts remain a concern, and a significant share of them traced back to knowledge content that was out of date.

Tom Becker leads Quality Assurance. Questions about scoring or calibration should go to him in the first instance.

## Evaluation Approach

QA analysts sampled interactions each week across voice, chat and email. Each agent received a minimum of four scored evaluations during the quarter. The standard scorecard was used without changes. It covers:

- Verification and data protection
- Accuracy of information given
- Ownership and resolution
- Escalation handling (when to escalate, and quality of handover notes)
- Tone and call control
- Correct wrap-up and disposition coding

Calibration sessions ran fortnightly with supervisors. Variance between scorers was within tolerance for most sections. Wrap-up coding had the widest spread and will be the focus of the first calibration session in Q2.

## Key Findings

**Escalation handling.** W. Lin ran the escalation-handling training for Tier 1 agents in Q1. Agents who completed the sessions showed fewer unnecessary escalations in later evaluations. Handover notes were more complete, and more of them included the troubleshooting steps already attempted. Some agents still escalated billing disputes that could have been resolved at first contact. The training material should address this next time.

**Repeat contacts and knowledge content.** Quality Assurance found that outdated Beacon articles caused 18% of repeat contacts in Q1. In these cases agents followed the article correctly, but the guidance no longer matched current products, pricing or process. Customers then had to call back. Common examples included:

- Superseded router reset steps
- Old tariff names and allowances
- Retired refund timescales

Because the agents followed the article as written, they should not be marked down on accuracy for these interactions. QA has adjusted the affected scores.

**Other observations.** Verification compliance remained high. Chat agents continue to close sessions without confirming that the issue is resolved. This was raised with chat supervisors mid-quarter.
